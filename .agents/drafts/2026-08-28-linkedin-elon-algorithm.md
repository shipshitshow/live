You never understood Elon Musk.

Karim Bousta cracked the system behind how he works.

Not from a book. From three years beside him, inside Tesla.

On Silicon Carne with Carlos Diaz, Karim did not offer another opinion about Musk. He explained how the man actually operates.

Musk calls it "the algorithm."

There is no playbook waiting on your desk on day one. Carlos laid out its five steps. Karim explained what happens when you actually run them.

Everything starts with first principles: do not inherit the previous process. Ask what is genuinely required.

1. Question every requirement

Why is this necessary?

Unless someone can prove you need it, challenge it. Every requirement needs an owner—not a department or team, but a person.

Karim repeated Musk's warning: the smarter the person, the more dangerous their bad requirements can become.

2. Delete everything you can

If something fails the test, do not improve it. Remove it.

Keep deleting until almost nothing remains. At Tesla, they would even ask why a car needed a steering wheel.

Skip this step and everything that follows merely makes the wrong machine faster.

3. Simplify and optimize

Only after the deletion.

Simplify too early and you polish work that should never have existed.

4. Accelerate

Now ask whether the process can run 10 or 100 times faster.

You cannot reach that speed while the process is still heavy. A year becomes two months. If you are Musk, perhaps two weeks.

5. Automate last

Only now should you automate.

People assume automation creates speed. It does not. It multiplies whatever already exists.

Automate a useless step and you lock it in. AI makes this dangerously easy: you can automate a process that makes no sense and serves nobody.

That is the method:

First principles.
Question.
Delete.
Simplify.
Accelerate.
Automate last.

Most teams start with AI.

That is the algorithm run backwards.

The good news? You can use Musk's method without becoming Elon Musk.
